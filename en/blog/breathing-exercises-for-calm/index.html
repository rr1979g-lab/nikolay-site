Breathing exercises for calm: 4 simple techniques

Nikolay Shcherbakov · Published: 2026-11-23

Your breath is the one calming tool you always carry with you. Here are four well-known techniques, explained step by step, with advice on when to use each and how to practise safely.

Why slow breathing helps you feel calmer

When we are stressed, breathing tends to become fast, shallow and high in the chest. When we are relaxed, it is slower and softer. The connection also works the other way: by deliberately slowing your breath, and especially by lengthening the exhale, you give your body a signal that it is safe to settle down.

Breathing practices are not a cure for anxiety disorders or a replacement for medical care. But as a daily habit and as a quick tool in a tense moment, many people find them genuinely helpful. Nikolay Shcherbakov has included breathwork in his personal practice for more than 20 years, and the techniques below are among the simplest to learn.

Before you start

Sit comfortably with a straight but relaxed back, or lie down. Breathe through your nose unless the technique says otherwise. Let your belly, not just your chest, move with the breath. Do not force anything: the counts are guidelines, and it is fine to make them shorter until the rhythm feels easy.

1. Box breathing (4-4-4-4)

Box breathing has four equal phases, like the four sides of a square. It is easy to remember and is a good choice when you need to collect yourself and stay focused, for example before a meeting or an exam.

  • Breathe in through your nose for a count of 4.
  • Hold your breath gently for 4.
  • Breathe out slowly for 4.
  • Hold with empty lungs for 4.
  • Repeat for 4–6 rounds. If the holds feel uncomfortable, start with a count of 3.

2. The 4-7-8 technique

The 4-7-8 method has a long exhale, which makes it a popular choice in the evening and when you want to unwind before sleep.

  • Place the tip of your tongue just behind your upper front teeth.
  • Exhale completely through your mouth.
  • Close your mouth and breathe in quietly through your nose for a count of 4.
  • Hold your breath for 7.
  • Exhale fully through your mouth for 8, with a soft “whoosh” sound.
  • That is one cycle. Start with 4 cycles and do not go far beyond that while you are learning. The pace of counting can be faster at first; what matters is keeping the 4:7:8 ratio.

3. Equal breathing

Equal breathing simply means making the inhale and the exhale the same length, without holds. It is the gentlest of the four and a good starting point if you are new to breathwork or if breath holding feels unpleasant.

  • Breathe in through your nose for a count of 4.
  • Breathe out through your nose for a count of 4.
  • Keep the transition between inhale and exhale smooth, without pauses.
  • After a minute or two, if it feels easy, try 5 and 5 or 6 and 6.
  • Continue for 3–5 minutes.

4. Coherent (resonant) breathing

Coherent breathing, also called resonant breathing, is slow, even breathing at about five to six breaths per minute. It is suited to longer sessions and is often practised daily as a calm, steady routine.

  • Breathe in softly through your nose for about 5–6 seconds.
  • Breathe out through your nose for about 5–6 seconds.
  • Keep the breath light and relaxed. It should not feel like you are taking huge breaths.
  • Practise for 5 minutes at first, then gradually up to 10–20 minutes if you like it.
  • A timer or a guide with a sound or visual cue makes it much easier to keep the rhythm.

Which technique to choose and when

Short, regular sessions build the habit faster than occasional long ones. Two or three minutes in the morning, a short pause during the day and a few minutes before sleep are a good start. A brief breathing pause is also a great replacement for reaching for your phone; see our tips on how to stop scrolling your phone.

  • Need to focus and stay steady under pressure: Box breathing.
  • Winding down in the evening or before sleep: 4-7-8.
  • New to breathing practice, or holds are uncomfortable: Equal breathing.
  • A daily calming routine of 5–20 minutes: Coherent breathing.
  • A tense moment in the middle of the day: a few rounds of whichever technique feels easiest to you.

How to fit breathing practice into an ordinary day

The best technique is the one you actually do. To make sure practice is not forgotten, attach it to familiar moments of your day. You do not need a special time or place: a few calm minutes are enough.

  • In the morning, before the phone: two minutes of Equal breathing sitting on the edge of the bed.
  • Before an important conversation or call: three or four rounds of Box breathing.
  • On public transport or in a queue: Coherent breathing with your eyes closed, if it is safe.
  • After a long stretch at the screen: a short pause with a long exhale, looking out of the window.
  • At bedtime: four cycles of 4-7-8 in bed with the lights off.

Safety notes

Breathing exercises are generally gentle, but listen to your body. If you feel dizzy, light-headed, short of breath or unwell, stop the exercise and return to normal, natural breathing. Do not practise while driving or in water.

If you have a heart or lung condition, are pregnant, or have any other health concerns, consult a doctor before starting breathing practices, especially techniques with breath holding, such as Box breathing and 4-7-8. Breathing practices support well-being but do not replace medical advice or treatment.

Questions and answers

What is the best breathing exercise to calm down quickly?

Many people find a long, slow exhale most calming, so 4-7-8 or a few rounds of Box breathing are good options. If breath holding is uncomfortable, simple Equal breathing works well too.

How do you do 4-7-8 breathing?

Breathe in through your nose for 4 counts, hold for 7, and exhale fully through your mouth for 8. Start with 4 cycles.

How long should I do breathing exercises?

Even 2–3 minutes can help in a tense moment. For a daily routine, 5–10 minutes is a comfortable target, and coherent breathing can be extended to 20 minutes if it feels good.

Is it normal to feel dizzy during breathing exercises?

Mild dizziness can happen if you breathe too deeply or too fast. Stop, return to natural breathing and next time use shorter counts. If dizziness is strong or repeats, consult a doctor.
