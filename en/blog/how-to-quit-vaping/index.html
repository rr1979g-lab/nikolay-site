How to quit vaping: a clear plan without beating yourself up

Nikolay Shcherbakov · Published: 2026-11-24

Vaping feels milder than smoking, so the habit quietly spreads: a puff in the car, at the desk, before bed. Let's look at why it is so hard to stop and build a step-by-step plan that helps you ride out cravings and stay away from the device.

Why quitting vaping is so hard

Most vape liquids contain nicotine, and the body gets used to it. But chemistry is only part of the story. A vape is always within reach and can be used almost anywhere, with no need to step outside or wait for a break. So puffs slip into dozens of small moments during the day, and your hand reaches for the device before you notice.

Vaping also tends to become a way of handling tension, boredom or a pause: starting a hard task, getting through an awkward conversation, "resetting". The habit grows strongest exactly where it eases a small discomfort. That is why a simple "I'll stop on Monday" is rarely enough. You need other ways to get through those minutes, prepared in advance.

Get ready: 3–5 days before your quit date

Good preparation makes the first days much easier. Pick a quit date within the next week or two, ideally a calm period without deadlines or travel, and do a few simple things before it:

  • Watch yourself. Note when and where you reach for the vape and what you feel just before: boredom, stress, tiredness, the urge to take a break.
  • Write down your reasons for quitting: money, freedom from the device, how you feel, setting an example for your kids. Keep the list handy.
  • Plan a substitute for each common situation: a glass of water, sugar-free gum, a short walk, a few slow breaths.
  • Tell the people close to you that you are quitting, and ask them not to offer you a vape or vape next to you for a while.
  • On quit day, remove the device, pods and liquids from your home and bag. The harder it is to "just grab it", the easier it is to let an urge pass.

Cold turkey or step by step?

Some people find it easier to stop in one day; others first cut down the number of puffs and the strength of the liquid, then stop. There is no single right answer. If you go step by step, set a final quit date right away, otherwise cutting down can drag on for months.

If you have vaped heavily for a long time or have relapsed before, talk to a doctor. They can explain what support options exist and whether they suit you. That is a sensible step, not a sign of weakness.

What to do when a craving hits

A craving comes like a wave: it builds, peaks and fades, especially if you don't feed it. The goal is not to crush it with willpower but to wait out a few minutes. A simple sequence helps:

  • Notice and name it: "This is a craving. It will pass."
  • Delay the decision: "I'll come back to this in ten minutes."
  • Take a few slow breaths with a longer out-breath.
  • Change your surroundings: stand up, drink some water, step outside for a minute.
  • Keep your hands and mouth busy: nuts, carrot sticks, gum, a pen, a stress ball.
  • Reread your reasons for quitting or text someone who supports you.

Breathing as a replacement for the puff

Vaping has one important feature: the puff itself is a slow, deep inhale followed by a long exhale. Part of the relief people feel comes from that rhythm. That is why breathing exercises make a good substitute: the same gesture of pausing, just without the device.

Try this: breathe in through your nose for a count of four, pause briefly, then breathe out slowly through your lips for a count of six. Repeat five to ten times. If you prefer to follow prompts, the Breath Prana Moment app offers Box Breathing, 4-7-8 and other techniques with voice guidance. A few more simple methods are collected in the article on breathing exercises for calm.

Breathe gently and never force it. If you feel dizzy or uncomfortable, stop and return to normal breathing.

Tricky situations: friends, coffee, stress

Slips rarely happen on an ordinary day. They tend to happen in particular situations, so think them through in advance:

  • Friends who vape. Let them know beforehand and keep a drink or something else in your hand. If it's hard, step away for a couple of minutes when everyone starts vaping during the first weeks.
  • Coffee and work breaks. Change the ritual: a different spot, a different drink, a short walk instead of a vape break.
  • Alcohol. It lowers your guard, so it's wise to keep it to a minimum in the first weeks.
  • Stress and tiredness. This is where ready-made substitutes matter most: breathing, water, five quiet minutes, a talk with someone close.

The first days and weeks

In the first days many people notice irritability, restlessness, trouble concentrating or the urge to snack. This is a common reaction to giving up a habit, and it usually eases over time. Simple things help: more water, regular meals, movement, sleep, less coffee in the evening.

Mark every vape-free day. Visible progress is one of the strongest motivators: after a week you already have something worth protecting. Think about small rewards too: the money that used to go on pods can be set aside for something you enjoy.

If you slip

One puff doesn't erase everything you have done. The real danger is not the slip but the thought "I've blown it anyway, so I might as well keep going". Calmly look at what happened: where you were, what you felt, what was missing. Add protection against that exact situation to your plan and return to it the same day. Many people quit on a later attempt, and every attempt teaches you something useful.

Questions and answers

How can I quit vaping on my own?

Pick a date, watch your triggers in advance, prepare substitutes for common situations and get the device out of your home. When a craving hits, ride out the wave: a few slow breaths, some water, a change of scene.

What can I use instead of vaping when the urge is strong?

Slow breathing with a long exhale, sugar-free gum, water, a short walk or something to keep your hands busy. Choose your substitute in advance so you don't have to invent one in the moment.

How long do vape cravings last?

A single wave usually passes within a few minutes if you don't feed it. Overall it differs from person to person; over time the waves usually become rarer and weaker.

Is it better to quit vaping cold turkey or gradually?

Both can work. If you cut down gradually, set a final quit date from the start. If you're unsure or have relapsed before, it's worth talking to a doctor.

What should I do if I relapse and vape again?

Don't beat yourself up or treat the attempt as a failure. Look at what led to the slip, add protection against that situation to your plan and carry on the same day.
