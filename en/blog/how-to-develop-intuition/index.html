How to develop intuition: 7 practical exercises

Nikolay Shcherbakov · Published: 2026-11-23

Intuition is not magic and not a gift for the chosen few. It is a skill of noticing quiet inner signals, and like any skill it grows with practice. Here are exercises you can start today.

What intuition is, in plain words

Intuition is the quick sense of an answer that comes before you have thought everything through. You meet a person and something feels off. You read a job offer and feel a quiet “yes”. You take a turn on the road and later realise you somehow knew it was the right one.

The problem is rarely that intuition is missing. Much more often, the first signal is drowned out a second later by doubts, fears, other people's opinions and endless analysis. So developing intuition is mostly about two things: learning to notice the first signal, and learning to tell it apart from the noise that follows.

Intuition, fear and wishful thinking: how to tell them apart

Before the exercises, it helps to know what you are looking for. In Nikolay Shcherbakov's experience of more than 20 years of practice, these differences are the most useful guide:

  • Intuition usually comes first, quickly and quietly. Fear and doubt arrive afterwards and argue loudly.
  • An intuitive signal feels calm and simple, even when it says “no”. Fear feels tense, hurried, and comes with a stream of “what ifs”.
  • Wishful thinking repeats what you want to hear. Intuition sometimes tells you something you would rather not know.
  • Intuition does not need long justification. Anxiety keeps building new arguments.

7 exercises to develop intuition

Do one or two a day for a few minutes. Regularity matters more than duration.

  • The three-second answer. When you face a small choice, give yourself three seconds and say your first answer aloud or write it down before you start thinking. Then decide as usual and compare.
  • Guess before you look. Before opening a message, guess who it is from. Before checking the weather, guess whether it will rain. Note your guesses and check them. This trains attention to the first impression in a safe, playful way.
  • The body check. Sit quietly, imagine one option and notice your body: does it feel lighter or heavier, open or tight? Then imagine the other option. The body often reacts before the mind has finished explaining.
  • The intuition journal. Every evening write down one moment when you had a hunch, what it said and what actually happened. After a few weeks you will see patterns: in which areas your intuition is accurate and when fear disguises itself as intuition.
  • The coin trick. If you cannot choose between two options, assign them to sides of a coin and toss it. In the moment it is in the air, or when you see the result, notice your reaction. Relief or disappointment shows what you really want.
  • Five minutes of silence. Intuition is hard to hear in constant noise. Spend five minutes a day without the phone, music or conversation, simply observing your breath and thoughts.
  • First impression of people. When meeting someone new, quietly note three words that describe your first impression. Revisit them a month later.

What drowns out your intuition

Sometimes exercises seem not to work, not because your intuition is “weak” but because it is constantly drowned out. Remove even one of these obstacles, for example by making a decision after a good night's sleep or in a quiet place, and your inner voice becomes clearer. The most common ones are:

  • Tiredness and lack of sleep. When you are exhausted, anxiety is louder than any quiet signal.
  • Hurry. When a decision must be made “right now”, we grab the first logical argument or someone else's opinion.
  • The need to please everyone. If other people's reactions matter most, you simply stop noticing your own sense.
  • Fear of making a mistake. The stronger the fear, the longer the list of pros and cons and the less room for the first impression.
  • Information overload. News, feeds and endless advice take up all your attention, leaving none for inner signals.

A simple daily routine

To make practice a habit, attach it to something you already do. In the morning, over coffee, make two or three guesses about the day. During the day, use the three-second answer for small choices: what to eat, which route to take, which task to do first. In the evening, spend five minutes with your journal.

This takes about ten minutes a day, and after a couple of weeks most people notice that they hear their first sense more clearly and second-guess themselves less.

When not to rely on intuition alone

Intuition is a valuable source of information, but not the only one. In matters of health, money, law and safety, combine your inner sense with facts, expert advice and careful thinking. A good rule: let intuition raise the question or point the direction, and then check it. Over time, the combination of a clear first signal and sober analysis is what makes decisions feel both confident and sound.

Questions and answers

How can I develop my intuition quickly?

Practise a little every day: give three-second answers to small choices, make guesses and check them, and keep an intuition journal. Short regular practice works better than rare long sessions.

How do I know if it is intuition or fear?

Intuition usually comes first, quickly and calmly, without long arguments. Fear comes afterwards, feels tense and brings a stream of “what if” thoughts.

Can intuition really be trained?

Yes, in the sense that you can learn to notice your first signals more clearly and check how accurate they are. Exercises and honest feedback, for example in the Intuition Trainer app, help with that.

Which book should I read about intuition?

Start with The Intuition Code for practical exercises on noticing inner signals. Open Up to Your Dreams and The Right Choice continue the topic for life choices and difficult decisions.

Should I make big decisions based on intuition?

Use intuition as one source of information, not the only one. For important decisions, especially about health, money or safety, check your inner sense against facts and expert advice.
